    Fall blowouts · Winterization

    Before the freeze.

    A sprinkler blowout uses compressed air to push water out of the irrigation lines, so less of it is left to freeze and crack pipes, fittings and heads over a Michigan winter.

    Summer. Every line holds water, and zone 3 is running.

    Why water in the lines matters

    Water expands as it freezes. Water left sitting in pipes, fittings, valves and heads can crack them, and the backflow preventer, which sits above ground, is especially exposed.

    The damage usually stays hidden until the system is turned back on in spring.

    What a blowout generally involves

    1. Shut off the water supply to the irrigation system.
    2. Connect a compressor at the system's blowout point.
    3. Clear each zone with controlled air pressure, one at a time, until the heads give off mostly mist.
    4. Drain above-ground parts like the backflow preventer as needed, and set the controller for winter.

    Every system is set up a little differently, and a blowout lowers the risk of freeze damage rather than ruling it out.
